Research statement

My research is motivated by the clinical and engineering challenges of congenital heart disease, especially single-ventricle physiology and the Fontan circulation. I develop tools that combine fluid mechanics, imaging, experiments, and machine learning to extract more useful information from cardiovascular systems and to inform the design of better devices and measurement platforms.

Across my projects, the central question is consistent: how can we make cardiovascular analysis more mechanistic, patient-specific, and translational? I address this through high-resolution flow reconstruction from sparse measurements, biofluid experiments for blood-contacting devices, and low-cost sensing systems for experimental cardiovascular research.

Featured research area · 4D Flow MRI reconstruction and pressure estimation

Featured direction

Physics-informed reconstruction of cardiovascular flow from sparse measurements.

In my current computational work, I use neural networks constrained by the Navier–Stokes equations to recover high-resolution cardiovascular flow fields from sparse data. This framework aims to enhance 4D Flow MRI by combining limited measurements with governing physics, anatomical constraints, and clinically relevant outputs such as pressure gradients.

  • High-resolution velocity reconstruction
  • Pressure-field inference for cardiovascular analysis
  • Patient-specific application to Fontan circulation
